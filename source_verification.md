## D. Artistic precedents (each checked on a primary page, 2026-10-20)

- **Ridler, *Myriad (Tulips)* (2018), annaridler.com.**
  - "thousands of hand-labeled photographs of tulips"; later the dataset for *Mosaic Virus*; "Computability is often accomplished by categorisation."
  - Lesson: datasets and labelling as exhibitable labour.
- **Chung, *Drawing Operations*, sougwen.com.**
  - 2014–present; Generation 2 (2017) uses "a neural network trained on two decades of Chung's archived drawings" and the artist's drawing gestures. *Collectivity* (2018) uses motion data from New York public surveillance feeds and asks "Where does 'I' end and 'we' begin?"
  - Lesson: co-creative performance; this project moves from one artist to many contributors.
- **Dinkins, *Not the Only One*, stephaniedinkins.com.**
  - Trained on oral histories from three generations of women in her family; "hosted on local computers to protect community data"; off-the-shelf datasets contain "deep bias against blackness".
  - Lesson: data custody as an artistic choice.
- **Herndon & Dryhurst, *The Call*, Serpentine North, 4 Oct 2024 – 2 Feb 2025.**
  - Fifteen UK community choirs recorded; choral models trained on the recordings; Data Trust experiment.
  - Lesson: governance of collective voice, designed by the artists and institution.
- **McCarthy, *LAUREN*, get-lauren.net.**
  - "I become a human Alexa"; exposes "the hidden role of human labor in an automated future". The page gives no year; its press dates from 2017.
- **Walshe, "13 Ways of Looking at AI, Art & Music," *Unsound Dispatch*, 15 Dec 2023.**
  - "AI is not a singular phenomenon."
  - Status: the primary URL loaded, and the quoted sentence was confirmed.
