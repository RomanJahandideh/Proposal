{\large\textbf{Proposed Plan of Study}}\\[2pt]
Roman Jahandideh --- PhD, Communication \& Culture (Fall 2027)\\
\textit{Whose Contribution Counts? AI Mediation and Collective Authorship in Digital Improvisation}
\end{center}

Generative AI is entering collaborative creative tools. In these tools a model
does more than produce content: it reinterprets what people contribute. When
one participant's phrase becomes a shape, a sound or a change in a shared
scene, others have to decide what that transformation means and whether it
still belongs to the person who offered it. My doctoral research asks how
participants, facilitators and a generative-AI interface together determine
whether a person's contribution is acknowledged, transformed, taken up,
contested and credited in a shared work. The question is about communication
and cultural participation: how authorship and the right to refuse are
recognised in collective production, and how interfaces and model defaults
shape whose expression counts.

My objects of study are small-group digital improvisation workshops with
adult improvising artists and creative-technology practitioners, and a
research system I will build for them. The system is a shared Unity
audiovisual environment. Participants submit short creative phrases, and an
existing language model proposes bounded, reviewable transformations into
spatial and sonic elements. Contributors or groups can accept, revise, reject
or undo each proposal, and anyone can take part without AI.

I plan to use the program's research-creation option. The methods are:
scoping interviews with improvisers and facilitators; co-design of the
system's permissions with practitioners; repeated workshops comparing
arrangements in which the contributor decides, the group decides, or a person
rather than a model transforms the material; and follow-up interviews in which
participants review episodes of their own sessions. I will analyse these
episodes through interaction analysis and reflexive thematic analysis. My
framework is Lucy Suchman's account of how agency is attributed and configured
in human--machine relations, complemented by critical improvisation studies'
treatment of co-creation as an ethical relation. I will treat system logs as
records of events, not as evidence of intention, and examine my own role as
designer and facilitator.

The project fits the Technology in Practice area, with Media \& Culture as my
minor. I hope to work with Dr.\ Rebecca Caines, whose research on improvisation
as a methodology for creative technologies, and on generative AI as an
improvisatory practice, aligns closely with this setting and approach.

My background may appear distant from communication and culture, so I want to
explain how it prepares me. I hold a Bachelor of Architecture (Tabriz Islamic
Art University) and a Master of Landscape Architecture (Istanbul Technical
University), where I studied perception in virtual environments; that work was
published at eCAADe 2023. I am completing a thesis-based master's degree in
Interactive Arts and Technology at Simon Fraser University. There I have
designed, built and evaluated multiplayer Unity systems, including an ambient
interface that lets remote teammates perceive one another's state without
emotion labels. That project led me to Science and Technology Studies and to
the questions of interpretation, privacy and control that motivate this
proposal. My two manuscripts from this work are under revision. I have
conducted ethics-approved mixed-methods studies, taught animation and
interactive media as a teaching assistant, and served on SFU's Senate Graduate
Studies Committee. Doctoral study would let me ground this practice in
communication and cultural theory.
