\begin{center}
  {\bf\large Proposed Plan of Study}\\[2pt]
  Roman Jahandideh --- PhD in Communication \& Culture, Fall 2027\\
  \textit{Whose Contribution Counts? AI Mediation and Collective Authorship in Digital Improvisation}
\end{center}

In collaborative creative tools, a generative model reinterprets what
participants contribute: one person's phrase becomes a sound or a moving form,
and its output may become more visible than the
contribution that prompted it. My doctoral research asks how participants,
facilitators and a generative-AI interface shape whether a person's
contribution is recognised, carries creative authority, and is credited in a
collective work. This is a question of AI-mediated communication: I study AI
not as a creative partner but as a medium that translates one person's offer
to others, and ask whether the system's record of who supplied what matches
how collaborators recognise one another. Table~1 shows where this extends
the closest studies.

My objects of study are small-group digital improvisation workshops with
adult improvisers, and the shared audiovisual environment I will build. A language model proposes transformations
of participants' phrases into light, form and sound, which contributors or
groups accept, revise or refuse. Using AI is always optional.

Under the research-creation option, the environment, improvised works and
workshop-tested scores form the creative component. Methods are scoping interviews, co-design with practitioners,
repeated workshops (provisionally three groups, four sessions each)
comparing contributor-led, group-led and human-mediated arrangements, and
video-based reflection interviews. The framework is Lucy Suchman's account
of how agency is figured at the human--machine interface.

The project fits Technology in Practice, with Media \& Culture as a minor. I
hope to work with Dr.\ Rebecca Caines, whose research on improvisation as
methodology, socially responsive creative technologies, and generative AI as
a critical improvisation practice fits this approach.

I come to communication and culture through design. Trained in architecture
(Tabriz Islamic Art University) and landscape architecture (Istanbul
Technical University), I have always been curious about what my designs do
to the people and communities who live with them. At Istanbul I studied
perception of virtual environments (eCAADe 2023). In my thesis-based
master's in Interactive Arts and Technology at Simon Fraser University, I
built multiplayer systems that let remote teammates sense one another's
heart-rate state without emotion labels, accepted at ACM ISS 2026.
Participants accepted that display only if they controlled who saw it.
That finding moved my question from what a design does to people to who
controls how a shared system interprets them. I have run ethics-approved
studies, worked as a teaching assistant, and served on SFU's Senate Graduate Studies Committee.
