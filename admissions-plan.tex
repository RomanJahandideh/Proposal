\begin{center}
  {\bf\large Whose Contribution Counts? Generative AI, Cultural Participation\\
  and Collective Authorship in Research-Creation}\\[2pt]
  {\small Proposed Plan of Study --- PhD in Communication \& Culture, Fall 2027\\
  Roman Jahandideh}
\end{center}

I am an artistic practitioner and researcher working through
research-creation, currently completing an MA in Interactive Arts and
Technology at Simon Fraser University after training in architecture and
landscape architecture. I build interactive 3D environments and multiplayer
systems that examine how technical mediation shapes relations among
participants. My doctoral
project asks how generative AI (GenAI) systems, including models, interfaces and the
platforms through which they are accessed, reorganize interpretive authority and
collective authorship in collaborative artistic practice. When a model turns
a participant's words, images or sounds into shared material, whose
interpretation becomes visible, whose contribution remains recognizable, who
can revise or refuse the transformation, and who is credited or held
responsible? These questions reach further: platform companies
shape much of this mediation, models may privilege, flatten or misrecognize
culturally situated expression, and a system's record of who supplied what
seldom settles credit or responsibility.

My objects of study are collaborative art-making with a limited set of GenAI
and non-AI tools, and the collaborative artworks and installation produced
through that process. Using the program's
research-creation option, the project begins with my own studio inquiry,
followed by consultation with paid practitioners; workshops with
about 18--24 adult interdisciplinary artists and community-engaged
practitioners in three cohorts, with participant observation;
semi-structured focus groups; and the co-creation of a room-scale
installation, proposed for exhibition at York, in which revisions, refusals
and contested credit remain visible. Analysis combines episode, thematic and
version analysis.
Participants may refuse GenAI at any point without losing standing in the
collaboration and retain negotiated authority over use, revision,
attribution and withdrawal.

The project would contribute a communication-and-culture account of how
GenAI redistributes interpretive authority, a research-creation method for
studying it, and an artwork that keeps those negotiations open to public
encounter. It is primarily situated in Technology in Practice and secondarily in
Media \& Culture, and fits the program's aim of ``the application of critical
theory and research.'' I hope to work with Dr.\ Rebecca Caines, whose
research on improvisation as methodology, socially responsive creative
technologies and GenAI as a critical improvisation practice grounds this
approach.

In architecture at Tabriz Islamic
Art University (Best Dissertation Award, 2016) and landscape architecture at
Istanbul Technical University, where I studied perception of mediated
environments (eCAADe 2023), I designed space as a medium of social life. In the
iViz Lab, my thesis asked what happens when a technical system translates an
ambiguous human state into a shared representation. I built a networked Unity
game that rendered teammates' heart rate and heart-rate variability as one
integrated ambient cue (accepted at ACM ISS 2026), and BioTerrarium, a world
whose fog and light respond to the player, evaluated in a completed
within-subjects study with 24 participants (manuscript under revision). Participants asked for visibility to be
optional and mutable, with an immediate off switch. The PhD extends that
question from bodily signals to cultural production.
