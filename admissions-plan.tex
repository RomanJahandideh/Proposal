\begin{center}
  {\bf\large Proposed Plan of Study}\\[2pt]
  Roman Jahandideh --- PhD in Communication \& Culture, Fall 2027\\
  \textit{Whose Contribution Counts? AI Mediation and Collective Authorship in Digital Improvisation}
\end{center}

Generative AI is entering the tools people use to make creative work
together. In these tools a model reinterprets what participants contribute:
one person's phrase becomes a sound or a moving form in a shared scene. Its
outputs may then become more visible or influential than the contributions
that prompted them. My doctoral research asks how participants, facilitators
and a generative-AI interface shape whether a person's contribution is
recognised, transformed, taken up, contested and credited in a collective
work. The question concerns communication and cultural participation: whose
interpretations gain authority, whose contributions remain recognisable, and
how authorship is negotiated when a machine takes part in creative exchange.

My objects of study are small-group digital improvisation workshops with
adult improvising artists and creative practitioners, and the shared
audiovisual environment I will build for them. Participants contribute short
phrases. An existing language model proposes transformations into light,
form and sound, and contributors or groups can accept, revise, reject or
undo them. Anyone can take part without using AI.

I plan to use the program's research-creation option. The environment and
the improvised works made with it form the creative component, and
developing its rules and workshop scores is part of the inquiry. The methods
are:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item scoping interviews with improvisers and facilitators;
  \item co-design with practitioners;
  \item repeated workshops comparing contributor-led, group-led and
        human-mediated arrangements;
  \item reflection interviews in which participants review video of their
        own sessions.
\end{itemize}
I will analyse selected episodes by linking talk, action and system records,
and analyse interviews thematically. The framework is Lucy Suchman's account
of how agency is figured at the human--machine interface, complemented by
improvisation studies' attention to listening and response.

The project fits the Technology in Practice area, with Media \& Culture as a
minor. I hope to work with Dr.\ Rebecca Caines, whose research on
improvisation as a research methodology, on socially responsive creative
technologies, and on generative AI in research-creation aligns closely with
this setting and approach.

My background may appear distant from communication and culture, so I want
to explain how it prepares me. I hold a Bachelor of Architecture from Tabriz
Islamic Art University and a Master of Landscape Architecture from Istanbul
Technical University. There I studied how people perceive virtual
environments, work published at eCAADe 2023. I am completing a thesis-based
master's degree in Interactive Arts and Technology at Simon Fraser
University. In it I built and evaluated multiplayer Unity systems, including
ambient cues that let remote teammates perceive one another's heart-rate
state without emotion labels. Two manuscripts from this work have received
revision requests from ACM venues. In that work, participants made their acceptance
of the display conditional on controlling who could see it and when, which
led me to the question of who controls how a shared system interprets people. I have conducted
ethics-approved mixed-methods studies, worked as a teaching assistant in
animation and interactive media, and served on SFU's Senate Graduate Studies
Committee. Communication \& Culture would let me ground this practice in
critical and cultural theory.
