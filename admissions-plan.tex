\begin{center}
  {\bf\large Plan of Research: AI-Mediated Communication in Collective Creative Practice}\\[2pt]
  \textit{Whose Contribution Counts? Generative AI, Cultural Participation and Collective Authorship in Research-Creation}\\[2pt]
  {\small Proposed Plan of Study, PhD in Communication \& Culture, Fall 2027\\
  Roman Jahandideh}
\end{center}

I am an artistic practitioner and researcher working through
research-creation, trained in architecture and landscape architecture and now
building interactive 3D and game environments. My doctoral project asks how
generative AI (GenAI), meaning the models, interfaces and platforms that own
them, is reorganizing cultural participation: who can make meaning with these
systems, whose contributions become visible, who can refuse a model's
interpretation, and who receives credit or answers for the result. Its
central question is: how does collaborative artistic practice with GenAI
redistribute participation, cultural voice and authorship, and how can
research-creation make those redistributions visible and contestable? The
stakes reach beyond any one tool. Interpretive authority is concentrating in
proprietary platforms, models can standardize culturally situated
expression, and artists' work is taken into training data without consent.

My objects of study are collective art-making with a selected ecology of
GenAI and non-AI tools, and the artwork it produces. Using the program's
research-creation option, the project has five phases: my own studio inquiry
into collaboration and GenAI; consultations with four to six practitioners;
a series of workshops with about 18--24 adult artists, performers, designers
and creative technologists in three cohorts, in which I conduct participant
observation; semi-structured focus groups with each cohort; and the
co-creation of a large collaborative artwork proposed for exhibition at
York's Markham Campus. Analysis combines thematic, episode and artifact
analysis. Every activity has a non-AI route.

The project would contribute a communication-and-culture account of how
GenAI mediation redistributes recognition and creative authority, a
research-creation method for studying it, and an artwork and protocols that
make those relations perceptible in public. It belongs to Technology in
Practice, with Media \& Culture as a minor, and to the program's aim of
promoting ``the application of critical theory and research.'' I hope to work
with Dr.\ Rebecca Caines, whose research on improvisation as methodology,
socially responsive creative technologies and GenAI as a critical
improvisation practice grounds this approach.

Each stage of my training asked how designed environments shape the way
people perceive and relate to one another. In architecture at Tabriz Islamic
Art University (Best Dissertation Award, 2016), I designed space as a medium
of social life, where thresholds and paths decide who gathers and is seen.
Landscape architecture at Istanbul Technical University extended this to
public space; in its Virtual Landscape Lab I studied how people perceive
mediated environments (eCAADe 2023). At Simon Fraser University's iViz Lab,
my thesis research moved from space to interfaces: I build multiplayer
worlds and physiological interfaces in Unity and C\# and study them with
participants, in work accepted at ACM ISS 2026. Participants accepted a shared
heart-rate cue only if they controlled who saw it, a communication question
about who controls how a system represents people. The PhD carries it into
collective artistic practice.
