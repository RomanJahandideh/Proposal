\textbf{Central question.} How do GenAI systems reorganize interpretive
authority and collective authorship in collaborative artistic practice?

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{SQ\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Translation and cultural legibility.} How do selected models,
        interfaces, defaults, moderation systems and facilitation
        arrangements amplify, standardize, misread, refuse or pass over
        participants' culturally situated contributions? (Studio records;
        workshop episodes; prompts and outputs.)
  \item \emph{Negotiation and governance.} How do collaborators negotiate
        uptake, revision, refusal, attribution, responsibility and decisions
        among contributors, the group, the artist-facilitator, models,
        platforms and institutions? (Episodes; group rules; focus groups.)
  \item \emph{Research-creation and public form.} How can collaborative
        artistic methods and a public artwork make these usually hidden
        distributions of interpretive authority perceptible, debatable and
        revisable? (Scores; versions of the artwork; focus groups.)
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Provisional argument.} GenAI does not simply add a nonhuman
collaborator to collective art-making; it may reorganize the conditions
under which contributions become legible, transformable, creditable and
contestable by redistributing interpretive authority among participants,
facilitators, models, platforms and institutions. The research may confirm,
complicate or reject this proposition, and treats refusal, silence, failure
and non-use as meaningful outcomes.

\textbf{Terms.} \emph{Participation} means taking part in decisions, not
only access or interaction. \emph{Recognition} (legibility) is whether others
perceive a contribution as meaningful and as someone's. \emph{Interpretive
authority} is the capacity to decide what a contribution means and becomes.
\emph{Attribution} or \emph{credit} is public acknowledgement;
\emph{authorship} is felt and negotiated responsibility for a work;
\emph{ownership} is a legal or contractual claim; \emph{responsibility} is
who answers for consequences; and \emph{technical provenance} is a system's
record of inputs. \emph{Culturally situated contributions} are languages,
references, idioms, memories, aesthetic conventions, performance practices or
community knowledge that participants themselves identify. The study does not
judge authenticity or treat anyone as representing a culture.
