I am an artistic practitioner and researcher working through
research-creation. Trained in architecture and landscape architecture, I now
build interactive 3D and game environments and study them with participants
in human--computer interaction research. This project asks how generative
artificial intelligence (GenAI), meaning the models, the interfaces and the
platforms that own them, is reorganizing who takes part in cultural
production, whose contributions become visible and who holds authority over
shared meaning. Its central question is: \emph{how does collaborative
artistic practice with GenAI redistribute participation, cultural voice and
authorship, and how can research-creation make those redistributions visible
and contestable?} Three subquestions ask how tools, defaults and facilitation
translate, standardize or misread culturally situated contributions; how
participants negotiate recognition, refusal, credit and responsibility among
themselves, the group, me, the models and the platforms; and what protocols
and artistic forms emerge that might support more accountable and culturally
plural cultural production. The design has five linked phases: my own studio
inquiry with a selected ecology of GenAI and non-AI tools; pilot
consultations with four to six practitioners; a series of collaborative
workshops with about 18--24 adult artists, performers, designers and creative
technologists in three cohorts, in which I conduct participant observation;
semi-structured focus groups with each cohort; and the co-creation of a
large collaborative artwork proposed for exhibition at York University's
Markham Campus. The project would contribute a communication-and-culture
account of how GenAI mediation redistributes recognition and creative
authority, a research-creation method for studying it, and an artwork and
protocols that make those relations perceptible in public.
