The study aims to contribute at five levels without presuming its findings.
\emph{Conceptually}, it would offer a communication-and-culture account of
how GenAI mediation redistributes recognition, cultural voice, creative
authority and responsibility in collective art-making. \emph{Empirically},
it would show how participants negotiate transformations, defaults, refusals
and credit across changing tools. \emph{Methodologically}, it combines studio
inquiry, participant observation, focus groups, episode analysis and public
co-creation. \emph{Artistically}, it would produce a substantial
collaborative artwork and documented scores and protocols that materialize
its questions. \emph{Practically}, it would propose situated principles for
collaborative GenAI art on consent, refusal, attribution, accessibility,
platform choice and shared governance, not universal design rules. Its
originality lies not in new software but in following the same
contributions across studio, workshop, focus group and exhibition. The
significance reaches beyond one artwork: cultural participation is
increasingly mediated by systems whose training sources, defaults,
moderation rules and ownership are not equally visible or contestable, and a
workshop and gallery offer a bounded site where those relations can be
experienced, negotiated and interpreted.
