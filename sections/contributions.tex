The project's primary originality is conceptual: it moves beyond the binary
question of whether a human or machine authored an output to examine how
interpretive authority, recognition, credit and responsibility are
distributed through interfaces, platform rules, facilitation and collective
practice. Its methodological contribution is to trace participant-defined
contribution trajectories across workshop episodes, reflective accounts,
version histories and exhibition without treating technical provenance as a
definitive record of authorship. Empirically, it would offer situated
evidence of how collaborators negotiate transformations, defaults, refusal
and credit; artistically, an installation and scores that keep revisions,
absences and parallel versions perceptible; and practically, situated
principles for consent, attribution and platform choice.
