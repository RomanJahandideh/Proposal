The framework connects three bodies of work, each tied to one scale of
analysis.

\textbf{Participation as power (interactional scale).} Jenkins and colleagues
define a participatory culture as one in which ``members believe their
contributions matter, and feel some degree of social connection with one
another'' \citep[3]{jenkins2006}. \citet{carpentier2016} asks more: whether
participants share power at the moments when decisions are made, which
separates participation from access and interaction. \citet{bishop2012}
cautions that participatory art is too often judged by ethical rather than
artistic criteria. Together they let me ask when a contribution is merely
received by a model and when its maker shapes what the group does with it,
without treating participation as good in itself.

\textbf{Mediation and infrastructure (institutional and platform scale).}
\citet{suchman2007} shows that agency is figured and attributed in situated
practice rather than residing in humans or machines. AI-mediated
communication research describes models that modify messages between people
\citep{hancock2020}; in collective art the message becomes shared work, and
the system modifying it belongs to a company that presents itself as a mere
``platform'' while shaping the terms by which it is judged
\citep{gillespie2010}. Terms of service, opacity, gallery conventions and my
facilitation also distribute visibility and decisions.

\textbf{Collective production and recognition (cultural scale).}
\citet{becker1982} treats art as the work of a cooperative network organized
by conventions, which decide whose contribution counts as artistic.
\citet[32]{hodson2019} shows that even when design is opened to citizens and
clients, ``the story is still told in terms of design-led collaboration''.
Model defaults may work in a comparable way, deciding whose styles become
legible and whose labour disappears into ``the AI''.

Research-creation and critical improvisation hold these scales together.
Research-creation makes the making a way of knowing \citep{chapman2012} and
can challenge the norms of the fields it crosses (\citealp[37]{loveless2019},
quoted in \citealp[255]{caines2023consultation}). Caines treats improvisation
as a method built on ``real-time decision-making with others, surprise,
supported and accountable risk taking, deep listening, and \ldots{} the
reconfiguration of mistake'' (\citealp[273]{caines2023consultation}; see also
\citealp{caines2021fragile}), and reads her own GenAI work as ``a kind of
critical improvisation practice'' marked by ``a constant cycle of navigating
power, adaptation, surprise, failure, and creation''
\citep[193]{caines2026steaming}. I ask how that cycle plays out among many
people whose material is one another's offers.
