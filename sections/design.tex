In the program's research-creation option, a substantial creative work and a
150--200-page dissertation together address the theoretical concerns
\citep{comcult_rc}. The five phases overlap and feed back into one another
(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).

\textbf{Phase 1: Studio inquiry.} Before recruiting anyone, I will spend a
sustained period investigating collaboration and GenAI through my own
practice, working with three or four tool families for text, image, sound or
spatial generation alongside non-AI procedures. I will examine each tool's
defaults, moderation, access and privacy conditions, data custody and terms
of service, keeping a reflexive journal, a decision log, an archive of
failures and refusals, and a version record while making exploratory works
and scores. This is artistic and critical inquiry
rather than benchmarking. It ends by choosing no more than two generative
modalities for the workshops; Unity remains the environment for spatial
assembly, not the scholarly contribution.

\textbf{Phase 2: Consultation.} Before ethics-approved data collection, four
to six paid practitioners will act as consultants to review draft scores and
the proposed accessibility, consent, refusal, attribution and
data-governance protocols. Their feedback will refine the research design
but will not be analysed as study data. Any pilot involving data collection
will occur only after ethics approval.

\textbf{Phase 3: Collaborative workshops.} About 18--24 adults will take part
in three cohorts of six to eight. Participants will be recruited because
their artistic or community-engaged practices involve language, place,
memory, identity, cultural reference or community knowledge, not because
they are expected to represent a demographic or cultural group. Open calls
in Toronto and York Region, excluding anyone I teach, will seek variation in
discipline, cultural and linguistic experience, and familiarity with GenAI.
The scale rests on information power \citep{malterud2016}, not
saturation \citep{braun2021saturate}, and on available honoraria, venue
capacity and analysis time. Each cohort attends three sequential workshops
of about two and a half hours, plus a focus group, with make-up sessions:
the first sets conventions and makes work without assuming AI is needed; the
second works through improvisational scores with the selected modalities
and AI/non-AI combinations; the third develops material for the
installation. Cohorts negotiate how contributions may be transformed,
whether GenAI must be used, what may be shown or removed, how credit is
assigned and how disagreement is preserved. Participants may accept, revise,
reject, fork or withhold a transformation, and may refuse GenAI at any point
without losing standing in the collaboration, retaining negotiated authority
over use, revision, attribution, exhibition and withdrawal. Through
disclosed participant observation, my field notes will record when I make
art, facilitate, observe or analyse.

\textbf{Phase 4: Focus groups.} Each cohort joins one semi-structured focus
group of 60--90 minutes on recognition, authorship, cultural legibility,
refusal, responsibility, platform authority and data governance. Group interaction is part of the data \citep{kitzinger1994}, but
confidentiality cannot be guaranteed in a group, as participants will be
told. Participants reflect without authoring the findings, so this is not
co-analysis; private follow-up conversations are offered.

\textbf{Phase 5: Collaborative installation.} The provisional form is a
room-scale, multichannel interactive installation assembled in Unity. The
installation will not merely visualize workshop records. Its spatial,
temporal, visual and sonic composition will operate as a second site of
inquiry: incompatible versions may coexist, refusals may interrupt the work,
and contributions may remain present without being absorbed into a single
polished output. Through this form, authorship appears as negotiated and
unresolved rather than as a settled list of inputs. Scores act as
propositions that participants test, failures reveal infrastructural
assumptions, revisions redistribute decision-making and visible absences
record refusal. Each cohort keeps an autonomous module with its own credit
and data rules and transformation history; only the shared spatial
structure is negotiated across cohorts. The preferred venue is the
first-floor Art Gallery at York's Markham Campus \citep{yorkmarkham2024}, but
it remains a proposal requiring institutional approval and resources, and
another York or partner venue may be used. Consent to exhibit is separate
from consent to research, no audience data will be collected without a
separately approved protocol, and the work will be documented for
examination and dissemination.
