The project follows the program's research-creation option: a substantial
creative work and a 150--200-page written dissertation that carries the
theoretical argument \citep{comcult_rc}. Its five phases overlap and feed
back into one another (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).

\textbf{Phase 1: Studio inquiry.} Before recruiting anyone, I will spend a
sustained period investigating collaboration and GenAI through my own
practice, comparing a deliberately limited ecology of three or four tool
families with non-AI processes. Criteria are artistic relevance, cost, data
and privacy terms, documentability, user control and known cultural
defaults. I will keep a reflexive
studio journal, a decision log, an archive of failures and refusals, and a
record of each tool's version and terms of service, and will produce a
series of exploratory works and scores. The phase ends by selecting no more
than two primary generative modalities for the workshops; Unity remains the
environment for spatial assembly and exhibition. It is artistic inquiry, not
a technology benchmark.

\textbf{Phase 2: Consultation and pilots.} Four to six adult interdisciplinary
artists, improvisers, community-engaged practitioners or creative
technologists will work as paid consultants. In pilot sessions they test and
critique draft scores and the accessibility, consent, refusal, attribution
and data-governance protocols. They are not research participants or
co-researchers; only the resulting protocol changes are recorded.

\textbf{Phase 3: Collaborative workshops.} Subject to feasibility, ethics,
honoraria and supervisory advice, about 18--24 adults will take part in three
cohorts of six to eight. The principal population is adult interdisciplinary
artists and community-engaged creative practitioners, recruited by open calls
in Toronto and York Region, excluding anyone I teach; discipline, GenAI
experience, age and cultural and linguistic background are sought as
variation, and the sample is not representative. These practitioners have
working vocabularies for collaboration, attribution and situated expression,
and adults can weigh public exhibition and contested data practices. The
scale rests on information power \citep{malterud2016}, not on statistical
power or declared saturation \citep{braun2021saturate}. Each
cohort attends four workshops of about two and a half hours: agreeing
conventions and making without assuming AI is needed; working with the two
selected modalities and with AI/non-AI combinations; deciding collective
rules for attribution, data, credit and decisions; reflecting on misreading,
standardization, surprise and failure; and developing materials and scores
for the installation. Anyone may accept, revise, reject, fork or withhold a
transformation without losing creative standing. I will conduct disclosed
participant observation, and field notes and the journal will record when I
make art, facilitate, observe or analyze. With consent I will collect
recordings, prompts and outputs, version histories, logs, artifacts and group
rules; a log records system events, not intentions.

\textbf{Phase 4: Focus groups.} Each cohort then joins one semi-structured
focus group of 60--90 minutes on recognition, authorship, cultural legibility,
refusal, trust, responsibility, platform authority, data governance and
desired changes. Focus groups make participants' interaction part of the
data \citep{kitzinger1994}, but confidentiality cannot be guaranteed within a
group, which participants are told beforehand. Participants reflect on the
work without sharing analytic authority, so this is not co-analysis; an
individual follow-up is offered to anyone who prefers to speak privately.

\textbf{Phase 5: Co-creation and public artwork.} The provisional form is a
room-scale, multichannel interactive installation assembled in Unity, in
which contribution trajectories, transformations, revisions, refusals,
absences, parallel versions and contested credit remain visible or audible
rather than being erased by a polished output; its modalities will be
narrowed in Phases 1 and 3. Making produces knowledge here in four ways:
scores act as propositions that workshops test; failures expose
infrastructural assumptions; revisions redistribute decision power; and the
installation holds unresolved conflicts open to public encounter rather than
illustrating written findings. Each cohort keeps an autonomous module with
its own credit and data rules, only the shared spatial structure is
negotiated across cohorts, and unresolved disagreements remain as parallel
versions rather than being forced into consensus. The work is proposed for
the first-floor Art Gallery at York's Markham Campus \citep{yorkmarkham2024}
or another York or partner venue, subject to approval and resources; no venue
is confirmed. Consent to exhibit is separate from consent to research, no
audience data will be collected, and the work will be documented for
examination.
